### 1:25-2:25 - Screen Recording: Drawdown

**Show:** LucidFlex Drawdown page and Apex EOD Drawdown Explained page.

On drawdown, this is much closer than some people may expect.

LucidFlex uses end-of-day trailing drawdown. The loss limit is recalculated from the highest closing balance, not from an unrealized intraday spike.

Apex's current EOD account also calculates its threshold at the end of the day. That means the current Apex EOD account should not be confused with Apex's intraday trailing account.

For NQ traders, EOD drawdown is generally easier to manage because a winning trade that temporarily runs up and then pulls back does not move the threshold during the trade.

I call the basic drawdown model a tie. But Lucid gets a slight simplicity advantage because EOD drawdown is central to the LucidFlex product, while Apex still offers separate EOD and intraday products. Make sure you know which Apex account you are buying.

**On-screen score:** Drawdown: Tie, slight simplicity edge to Lucid

### 2:25-3:20 - Screen Recording: Passing the Evaluation

**Show:** Account tables on both official evaluation pages.

Apex wins the evaluation-speed category.

The current Apex EOD evaluation has no consistency rule and no minimum number of trading days. Apex says it may be passed in one trading day if the profit target is reached without breaking the rules.

LucidFlex has a 50% consistency requirement during the evaluation. Lucid says the structure allows the evaluation to be passed in two days, but it still prevents one day from representing too much of the total profit.

LucidFlex does give you a one-time purchase with no monthly rebilling, no fixed deadline to pass, and no activation fee after you pass. Apex gives you 30 days of evaluation access, and a passed account must be activated within seven calendar days by paying the one-time PA activation fee.

So if your only goal is the fastest possible pass, Apex wins. If you want unlimited time and no funded activation fee, Lucid has the cleaner cost structure.

**On-screen score:** Fastest possible pass: Apex

### 3:20-4:35 - Screen Recording: Funded Rules

**Show:** LucidFlex Funded Account page and Apex EOD PA page.

This is where Lucid becomes more attractive for me.

LucidFlex funded accounts currently list no daily loss limit, no funded consistency rule, no payout buffer, and a 90/10 payout split. The account still has its maximum loss limit, so risk management is not optional, but there are fewer separate rules to monitor every day.

Apex EOD Performance Accounts use tier-based position sizing and a tier-based daily loss limit. Reaching the daily loss limit pauses trading for the rest of the session instead of closing the account, but it is still another number the trader must manage.

Apex also applies a 50% consistency requirement for payouts and keeps a lifetime safety-net balance equal to the drawdown amount plus $100.

For a fast market such as NQ, fewer overlapping limits make account management easier. Lucid wins the funded-rule category.

**On-screen score:** Simpler funded rules: Lucid

### 4:35-5:45 - Screen Recording: Payouts

**Show:** LucidFlex Payouts and Apex EOD Payouts pages.

The payout comparison needs some nuance.

LucidFlex pays the trader 90% and keeps 10%. It requires five separate profitable trading days during each payout cycle. On a 50K account, the current minimum profitable day is $150. There is no funded consistency rule, no buffer requirement, and no fixed payout window. Once eligible, you can request on any day. The minimum request is $500, and payout caps apply.

Apex pays 100% of an approved EOD payout. It also requires five qualifying days, but a 50K account currently needs at least $250 on each qualifying day. Apex applies the 50% consistency rule and the permanent safety net. Each EOD PA is limited to six approved payouts, with a maximum amount for each request.

So Apex wins the payout-split percentage. Lucid wins for fewer payout restrictions. Which one matters more depends on how you trade.

**On-screen score:** Split: Apex | Rule simplicity: Lucid

### 5:45-6:35 - Screen Recording: Account Scale

**Show:** Apex EOD PA FAQ and Lucid Maximum Number of Accounts page.

Apex clearly wins if your strategy is to trade a large group of copied accounts.

Apex currently allows up to 20 active Performance Accounts, and it does not limit the number of evaluation accounts.

Lucid's current policy allows up to 10 total active accounts across evaluations and funded accounts, with no more than five funded accounts per household.

Most beginners should not choose a firm only because it lets them buy more accounts. But for an experienced trader with a proven copier setup, Apex's larger account allowance is a meaningful advantage.

**On-screen score:** Account quantity: Apex

### 6:35-7:25 - Screen Recording: NQ Risk Example

**Show:** Futures Prop Edge risk calculator with NQ selected.

Now let us put this into NQ terms.

One NQ contract is $20 per point. A 25-point stop is $500.

On a 50K account with a $2,000 maximum loss limit, that one trade risks 25% of the entire drawdown cushion. Two full losses would consume half of it.

That is why the name "50K account" can be misleading. Your practical trading account is the drawdown cushion, not the headline balance.

Whichever firm you choose, MNQ may be the better starting instrument until you build enough cushion. You can use the free calculator linked below to test your own stop size and contract count.

### 7:25-8:20 - On Camera: Final Verdict

Here is my final verdict.

For the average NQ or MNQ trader who wants the cleaner funded experience, I would choose LucidFlex. The combination of EOD drawdown, no funded daily loss limit, no funded consistency rule, no payout buffer, no activation fee, and no fixed payout window makes the account easier to understand and manage.

I would choose Apex if I wanted to scale across as many as 20 Performance Accounts, wanted the possibility of passing an evaluation in one day, or cared most about receiving 100% of approved payouts.

Apex is not a bad firm. It simply has more funded-account conditions to track. Lucid is my overall winner for rule simplicity, while Apex wins for account quantity and evaluation speed.
